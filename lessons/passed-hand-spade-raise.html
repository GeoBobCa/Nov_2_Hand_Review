How should a passed hand invite in spades?

A limit raise asks opener to decide whether the partnership has enough for game. You’ll make that decision from both seats, starting with what a passed hand can show when partner opens 1♠.

West deals. Both sides are vulnerable.

♠ AQ9 · ♥ 84 · ♦ 853 · ♣ AJ972

Choose a call. How many trumps do you have, and do you want to sign off, invite, or commit to game?

Reveal the bidding discussion and recorded result

Bid 3♠, a natural limit raise. You have 11 HCP, three spades headed by A–Q, and a side ace. Treat the hand as invitational. The small doubleton can help, but the flat 5–3–3–2 shape and lack of red-suit honours give little reason to upgrade. In standard SAYC, the direct limit raise can be made with three or more trumps. A direct 2♣ is a natural club bid. ACBL SAYC System Booklet

Rule of thumb. With three or more trumps and roughly 10–11 dummy points, raise a one-major opening directly to three of the major to invite game. The earlier pass helps opener judge your upper limit.

Common exception. With only two trumps, use a natural response appropriate to your hand. The limit raise promises at least three.

Recorded auction. North raised to 3♠ (Explore), and South bid 4♠ (Clarify). The pair’s actual agreement for 3♠ is unconfirmed. Reading it as a SAYC limit raise, South should pass with 11 HCP, five spades headed by the jack, and no singleton or void. The fourth-seat opening is already light in high cards, and the four-card heart suit gives little reason to accept on its own.

Traveller. South played 4♠ down three. It was the only spade game among 16 tables. Five tables stopped in 3♠ and all went down. Two played 2♠ and one made it. Those results show how this deal played across the field. North’s invitation and South’s acceptance are two decisions to assess separately.

Variation · One more trump

Same auction and vulnerability. Replace the ♥4 with the ♠4.

♠ AQ94 · ♥ 8 · ♦ 853 · ♣ AJ972

What does the fourth spade change? Would you still invite?

Reveal the variation

3♠ is still a reasonable invitation. The extra trump and singleton heart make this hand stronger in support. A fourth-seat opener can be light, so there is still value in letting South decide whether to bid game.

Independent check

You passed initially. Partner opens 1♠ in fourth seat and the next player passes. You have invitational values and exactly three spades. What do you bid in standard SAYC?

Check your answer

3♠. It is a natural limit raise, and three trumps are enough. Opener can pass with a minimum.

Alternative Conventions

One-way Reverse Drury: After a third- or fourth-seat major opening, a passed hand can use artificial 2♣ to show a good raise with at least three trumps. Opener can stop in 2♠ with a minimum.

Two-way Drury: One common agreement uses 2♣ for a good raise with exactly three trumps and 2♦ for a good raise with four or more. Pairs playing Drury should also agree on the meaning of a direct 3♠. Larry Cohen’s Drury notes
